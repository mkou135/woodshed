Woodshed · engine reference

How the engine works

A transcribed solo goes in. A ranked menu of things worth practising comes out. Four stages, three detectors, and one rule that shapes all of it.

Every number on this page is a copy. docs/ENGINE_SPEC.md is the source of truth, and the project rule is to re-read it rather than quote a parameter from memory. This page explains the shape of the machine — which decisions exist, in what order, and why. When you need a value, go to the spec.

The shape of it

src/pipeline.ts

One function, run(bytes), chains four stages. Each one hands the next a plain data structure and nothing else — there is no shared mutable state and no stage reaches backwards.

1

Ingest

src/ingest/

MusicXML in, a Score out: notes with onsets in ticks, a chord track, time signature, key, instrument, repeats and rehearsal marks. Repeats are expanded here, so downstream code sees the music as played rather than as printed.

One rule matters more than it looks: chord quality comes from the MusicXML <kind> element, never the chord symbol's text. A chart that prints “C7alt” tells you what the engraver typed; <kind> tells you what they meant. Text is a fallback for charts that have nothing else, and it is marked lower-confidence when used.

2

Prepare

src/prepare/

This stage reads the score and reports everything suspicious about it. It never edits. Its output is a CleanupReport: a list of Adjustment records saying what looks wrong, how severe it is, and why — the notices you see under the header when you run npm run solo.

Who is soloing The named region with the most notes wins. Two named regions raise a blocking notice — the engine will not guess between them.
What the form is Smallest repeating chord period; chorus phase from rehearsal letters, else double bars. Feeds the chorus-start prior in segmentation.
What looks off Unmarked pickups, out-of-range notes, chords that persist implausibly, long empty stretches, suspect repeats.

The reason for the no-edit rule is auditability. If the engine silently repaired a pickup bar, every downstream beat position would shift and nothing would say so. Instead it says so, loudly, and leaves the score alone.

3

Analyse

src/analyse/

This is the engine proper. It cuts the solo into phrases and ideas, works out what each note is doing against the chord under it, runs three independent detectors over the result, and merges what they found into a ranked list of findings.

Segmentation — where the lines break

Every gap between consecutive notes gets a boundary score built from three cues: the rest after the note, how long the note is held relative to the solo's median, and the size of the leap into the next note. Cross a threshold with a real rest present and the phrase ends there.

boundary = min(1, wrest·rest + wlength·length + wleap·leap) ← weights and threshold in ENGINE_SPEC §Segmentation

Two levels come out of this. A phrase is breath-to-breath. An idea is a gesture inside a phrase, opened by a weaker signal — an arrival, a local peak in the boundary profile, a pickup gesture, or a riff restatement. Ideas are the unit practice is cut into.

Several refinements exist because your ear disagreed with the corpus and won: riff binding keeps a chain of restatements as one phrase; the pickup gesture opens an idea at the anacrusis rather than the downbeat; a chorus-start prior nudges the boundary at a chorus downbeat rather than forcing one. Each of those is a recorded decision with a measured cost against the Weimar corpus.

Context — what each note is doing

Every note is given a degree relative to the chord sounding under it, using a major-family or minor-family table. “Chromatic” means altered and not a chord tone, so the b7 of a dominant is not chromatic. Separately, each chord is assigned the scale it is played on — never inferred from the notes played. The chart wins where it declares tensions; otherwise the choice follows the chord's function, not its quality.

That “never inferred” is load-bearing. Four different ways of inferring departure from pitch content were measured against null models on the corpus, and all four fired at or below chance. The engine reports what the chart says and lets you hear the rest.

The four detectors

They run independently and are allowed to disagree. None of them may match across an idea boundary — a figure that straddles a gesture break is two gestures, not one piece of vocabulary. The one exception is the resolution detector, whose two notes may cross a boundary when the gap between them is shorter than a rest: a resolution is legato or it is not one.

Shapes

Matches the played degrees against a hand-written dictionary of named figures — digital patterns, arpeggios, bebop cells. Longest match wins; a shorter hit inside a longer one is dropped.

detectors/shapes.ts

Targets

Finds short windows aiming at a goal note — approaches and enclosures — and scores the target's strength from beat position, relative length, harmonic arrival and which degree it lands on.

detectors/targets.ts

Recurring

Finds interval shapes the player returns to, names them by their intervals when the dictionary can't, and groups bent or inverted forms into variant families.

detectors/recurring.ts

Resolutions

Coker's 7-3 resolution: the b7 of one chord falling a half or whole step to the 3 of the chord a fourth above. The only detector whose subject is a chord change rather than notes against a chord; named from the pair of qualities (ii–V, V–I, V–i, V-of-V).

detectors/resolutions.ts

Confidence — why agreement is the signal

A finding's confidence is built mostly from how many independent detectors saw it, with bonuses for being nameable from the dictionary, for recurring more than once, and for the chord track being trustworthy — and a discount for being a bare triad. Below a floor, findings are dropped: a menu of twenty-six with a tail of two-note approaches is a menu nobody reads.

confidence = shortCellFactor × min(1, detectorCredit + namedBonus + repeatBonus + chordConfidence)

4

Practice units

src/practice/

An idea becomes a practice unit — the thing you actually drill. Findings name what is inside it. Long ideas are split into parts of a couple of bars so a unit stays playable.

Units are ranked, and the ranking is where the engine states an opinion. It rewards the strongest finding in the unit, the total across all its findings, how often those recur elsewhere in the solo, landing on a chord tone, and being nameable at all — then subtracts a penalty for being stock.

rank = 4·best + Σconfidence + 0.25·Σoccurrences + (chord-tone arrival) + (nameable) − stockPenalty·stock

“Stock” is the engine's way of not wasting your time on everyone's vocabulary. It measures three things — how much of the unit sits inside a run, how common its shape is across the Weimar corpus, and how much of it matches the mined lick table — and demotes by the larger of the first two. A run is four or more notes moving in one direction, whatever the interval sizes; a repeated note breaks it. Notes inside a named four-note cell are exempt, because that is the vocabulary; a bare triad is not exempt, because a triad is stock by definition. This penalty exists because of a real failure: the engine once passed 156 tests while ranking its best finding ninth out of eighty-one.

The run rule is the first part of the rank to have been checked against human labels. The Weimar annotators marked every idea in the corpus as a lick (vocabulary) or a line (a scale or arpeggio run), some fifteen thousand judgements the engine had never read. Scored against them, the old rule — which also required a run to keep one interval kind, all steps or all thirds — separated line from lick at about 0.71 in every length bin; direction alone reaches 0.84 on the shortest units, which is where the page's “mostly a scale run” label matters most. Length is the annotators' strongest cue of all, but a line is partly defined as a long run, so length stays out of the rank. Baker's rule for the bebop scale — chord tones on the downbeats — turned out to be no help at all: it is how a line is built, not what makes it a line rather than a lick. npm run eval:stock prints the table; it is a report, not a gate.

The four steps

Every unit carries the same four, drawn from the practice literature:

Loop it

The line exactly as played, with the chords that were under it. Sing it, then play along with the record until the time feel matches.

Through the tune

The same line over every chord in this solo's changes that will take it, plus a cycle-of-fourths drill.

Vary it

New ways into the same arrival — the landing note never moves. Approach ramps, displacement, fragments.

Write your own

A template with the targets marked. What you write can be dropped back into the app to be checked.

The agent layer

src/agent/

Optional, and deliberately boxed in. The agent judges; it never generates. It may weigh evidence the engine computed and cast a judgement — rank the menu, adjudicate a boundary the engine could not call, name something, narrate it — but every note, count and interval comes from deterministic code, and every verdict references engine objects by id.

This is why the boundary-candidate carets exist on the score: they are the gaps whose cue landed too near the threshold for the engine to be sure, handed to the agent as the only questions it is allowed to answer about where lines break. Without a key, the engine decides alone and nothing downstream notices.

The corpora

Three external bodies of music inform the engine, and none of them is in this repository. They live in ~/dev/woodshed-data/, are run locally, and only derived aggregate statistics are ever committed — with an attribution note. The licensing rule is the project's sharpest constraint.

  • Weimar Jazz Database (456 solos, ODbL) — the ground truth for phrase and idea boundaries, and the source of the corpus-frequency table behind the stock penalty. npm run eval:wjd scores segmentation against it; npm run eval:stock scores the stock signals against its lick/line labels; npm run corpus:wjd sweeps the whole pipeline and diffs against a committed golden of per-solo counts.
  • Bopland licks — a dictionary-coverage benchmark and second frequency source, mined into the common-language table.
  • Your own annotations in annotations/ — the only human-labelled evidence that reflects your ear rather than someone else's. Where these and the corpus disagree, recorded decisions say which won and what it cost.

The rules that shape everything

src/ is DOM-free; only app/ touches the DOM.

Enforced by the src-only tsconfig having no DOM library at all — it is a compile error, not a convention.

The Score is immutable.

prepare/ emits descriptions of what should change and never changes it, so nothing shifts under you silently.

The agent judges, never generates.

Every note, count and interval is deterministic. Verdicts point at engine objects by id.

Chord quality comes from <kind>, never text.

The markup says what was meant; the text says what was typed.

External corpora never enter the repo or the bundle.

Derived statistics may, with attribution. Test fixtures are hand-authored rather than quoted.

Green tests are not evidence the output is good.

Run the pipeline on a real solo and read what comes out. The spec names the expected result for the Blake transcription precisely so this is checkable.
